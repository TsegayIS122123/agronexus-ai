Q: What is teff rust and how do I treat it?
A: Teff rust is a fungal disease that appears as orange/brown pustules on leaves. Treatment: Remove infected plants, apply approved fungicides (e.g., Tilt 250EC), and ensure proper spacing for air circulation. Prevention: Use resistant varieties and rotate crops.

Q: How do I control wheat smut?
A: Wheat smut is caused by infected seeds. Treatment: Use certified disease-free seeds, treat seeds with hot water (52°C for 10 minutes), and practice crop rotation. Remove and destroy infected plants.

Q: When is the best time to plant teff in Ethiopia?
A: The optimal planting time for teff varies by region. In central Ethiopia (Shewa), plant between July and August. In northern Ethiopia (Tigray), plant between June and July. Ensure soil temperature is at least 15°C and moisture is adequate.

Q: How much fertilizer should I apply to wheat?
A: Apply 100-150 kg/ha of DAP at planting, and 100-200 kg/ha of urea in split applications (50% at tillering, 50% at booting). Always test soil first for optimal results.

Q: What are the signs of coffee leaf rust?
A: Coffee leaf rust appears as yellow-orange spots on the underside of leaves. Severely affected leaves drop off, reducing yield. Treatment: Apply fungicides (copper-based or systemic), maintain proper shade (40-50%), and prune affected branches.

Q: How do I store maize to prevent post-harvest loss?
A: Maize storage tips: Dry maize to 12-13% moisture content, use hermetic storage bags (PICS bags), store in well-ventilated areas, treat with approved insecticides, and regularly inspect for pests.

Q: What is the recommended spacing for maize in Ethiopia?
A: Maize spacing recommendations: 75cm between rows and 25-30cm between plants (approximately 53,000 plants per hectare). Use 60cm between rows with 2 plants per hill for high-yielding varieties.

Q: How can I improve soil fertility naturally?
A: Natural soil fertility improvement: Use compost and manure, practice crop rotation with legumes (e.g., faba beans), use green manure cover crops, and apply lime to acidic soils.

Q: What are common pests affecting teff and their control?
A: Common teff pests include: Cutworms (control: early planting, plowing), Armyworms (control: pesticides like Karate), and Rats (control: traps and bait). Regular monitoring is essential.

Q: When is the optimal time to harvest wheat?
A: Wheat harvest timing: When the crop is fully mature and dry (12-14% moisture), generally 4-5 months after planting in Ethiopia. Use combine harvesters for large areas.

Q: What is the best irrigation method for smallholder farmers?
A: Smallholder irrigation options: Drip irrigation (most water-efficient), Furrow irrigation (simple and low-cost), and Sprinkler irrigation (good for irregular terrain). Drip irrigation is recommended for high-value crops.

Q: How do I form a farmer cooperative?
A: Farmer cooperative formation steps: 1. Identify 10+ interested farmers, 2. Agree on purpose and by-laws, 3. Register with the Ministry of Trade, 4. Open a bank account, 5. Begin collective buying/selling.

Q: What are the market prices for teff in Ethiopia?
A: Teff market prices vary by region and season. As of 2024, average prices: Shewa 3,500-4,500 ETB/quintal, Amhara 3,200-4,000 ETB/quintal, Tigray 3,500-4,200 ETB/quintal. Check ECX daily prices for current rates.

Q: How can I get agricultural loans in Ethiopia?
A: Agricultural loan options: Commercial Bank of Ethiopia (CBE) agricultural loans, Development Bank of Ethiopia (DBE) agribusiness loans, Microfinance institutions (e.g., ACSI, Omo), and Cooperative banks. Prepare a business plan and collateral.

Q: What are the benefits of intercropping?
A: Intercropping benefits: Better land utilization, improved soil fertility (legumes fix nitrogen), reduced pest and disease incidence, crop diversification (risk management), and increased total yield per hectare.

Q: How do I control weeds in maize fields?
A: Weed control in maize: Manual weeding 2-3 times during the growing season, use recommended herbicides (e.g., Atrazine, Primextra) at pre-emergence, practice proper spacing to reduce weed competition.

Q: What are the signs of nutrient deficiency in plants?
A: Nutrient deficiency signs: Nitrogen (yellowing older leaves), Phosphorus (purple coloring), Potassium (yellow/brown leaf edges), Zinc (white/yellow spots, stunted growth), Iron (yellowing between leaf veins).
